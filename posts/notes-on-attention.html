Notes on Attention

Attention is not a resource you have. It is a thing you do. That distinction sounds pedantic until you notice how differently it makes you behave.

If attention is a resource, the goal is to conserve it, to ration it, to protect it from thieves. If attention is an act, the goal is to practice it, the way you would practice an instrument. You get better at it. You also get worse at it, if you stop.

Three observations

  • Quality beats quantity. Twenty minutes of complete focus will outproduce three distracted hours, and it will feel better too.
  • Attention is contagious. Sit next to someone who is deeply absorbed in their work and you will find it easier to become absorbed in yours.
  • The first five minutes are the hardest. The mind resists, invents errands, suddenly remembers an email. Wait it out. The resistance passes, and on the other side is the work.

What we attend to, we become. Choose carefully.
