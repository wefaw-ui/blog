On Building Slowly

There is a particular kind of pressure that comes from watching everything around you accelerate. Releases ship weekly. Opinions form in minutes. The feed refreshes before you have finished reading the last thing it showed you. In that environment, slowness looks like failure.

I have started to believe the opposite. The things I return to, the tools, the books, the objects on my desk, were almost all made by people who refused to hurry. Not because they were lazy, but because they understood that some decisions only become clear with time.

The cost of speed

Speed is not free. Every shortcut is a loan against the future, and the interest compounds quietly. A feature rushed out becomes a feature maintained forever. A sentence written in haste becomes a sentence someone misreads.

The work you do slowly is the work that lasts. Everything else is weather.

None of this is an argument against momentum. Momentum matters. But momentum and haste are different things. One is steady and deliberate; the other is anxious and scattered.

A practice

So I am trying something. One project, worked on a little each day, with no deadline. No announcement, no roadmap, no public progress. Just the work, and the patience to let it become what it wants to be.

I will report back. Eventually.
