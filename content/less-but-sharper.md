Dieter Rams gave us "less, but better." I want to propose a small amendment for writing: less, but sharper.

Most drafts are too long, not because the writer has too much to say but because they have not yet decided what they are saying. Length is often a symptom of uncertainty. We circle the idea because we have not found its center.

## Finding the edge

The editing process I trust most is brutal and simple:

1. Read each sentence on its own.
2. Ask whether the piece would be *worse* without it. Not different. Worse.
3. If the answer is no, it goes.
4. Repeat until nothing else can leave.

What remains is usually a third of the original, and it is almost always better. Not because short is inherently good, but because every surviving sentence now has to carry weight, and the reader can feel that.

> A sharp sentence cuts cleanly. A dull one just bruises.

This essay went through that process. It used to be four times as long.
